## 2. Warm-up period

The line starts empty and the supply releases cells faster than welding can process them, so buffers fill for hours before the system reaches steady state. MSER truncation on the replication-averaged cycle time puts the end of that transient at **3.75 h**. The original model used a 1 h warm-up, which biased every KPI towards the empty-line start; the scenario now uses 4 h of warm-up and measures 8 h (one shift).

## 4. What-if comparisons (vs baseline, paired)

| Change | Throughput Δ (/h) | Cycle time Δ (min) | WIP Δ | Throughput change significant? | Bottleneck after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|---|
| Add a 2nd laser welder | +6.0 ± 1.2 (+9.3%) | -6.4 ± 0.6 | -5.2 ± 0.4 | yes | Cell Stacking |
| Double weld infeed buffer (6 → 12) | +0.05 ± 0.04 (+0.1%) | +5.3 ± 0.2 | +5.8 ± 0.2 | yes | Laser Welding |
| Add a 2nd maintenance technician | +0.05 ± 0.05 (+0.1%) | +0.1 ± 0.2 | +0.2 ± 0.2 | no | Laser Welding |
| 2nd welder + 3rd stacking cell | +13.8 ± 2.1 (+21.5%) | -16.2 ± 0.8 | -15.9 ± 0.5 | yes | Cell Prep & Sorting |

Reading:

- **A bigger buffer in front of the bottleneck does not raise output.** Welding is never starved, so the extra slots only hold more parts: WIP and lead time rise and throughput stays flat (Little's Law: L = λW).
- **A second technician does nothing** because simultaneous breakdowns are rare.
- **A second welder lifts output** until stacking becomes the constraint. Relieving both stations moves the bottleneck upstream to cell prep: the classic shifting-bottleneck pattern.

## 5. Optimization (Optuna TPE + confirmation)

Decision variables: machines per station (current ± 1) and technicians (1–3). Objective: hourly profit with margin €180/module, €45/h per machine, €48/h per technician. 30 trials × 3 replications, then the top candidates were re-run (and trimmed) on 6 fresh seeds to remove the optimistic bias of picking the best of many noisy estimates.

| | Baseline | Best |
|---|---:|---:|
| Profit (€/h) | 10420 ± 340 | 13518 ± 631 |
| Throughput (/h) | 60.5 ± 1.9 | 78.4 ± 3.5 |
| Cost (€/h) | 463 | 601 |

**Changes:** `resources.technicians` 1 → 2, `station.stack.servers` 2 → 3, `station.weld.servers` 1 → 2.

**Confirmed gain:** +3098 ± 502 €/h (statistically significant).

Contribution of each change (best configuration vs the same configuration with only that change undone, paired on the confirmation seeds):

| Change | Profit effect of this change alone (€/h) | Significant? |
|---|---:|:---:|
| `resources.technicians` 1 → 2 | +72 ± 182 | no |
| `station.stack.servers` 2 → 3 | +1425 ± 452 | yes |
| `station.weld.servers` 1 → 2 | +3191 ± 511 | yes |

The welder and the stacking cell carry the gain. The extra technician is marginal: positive on average because the optimized line has more machines that can fail at the same time, but not statistically distinguishable from zero. A manager could reasonably skip it.

|---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|---|

Reading: the center ships about as fast as orders arrive (180/h), so an extra packing bench adds little throughput. It does cut order lead time and WIP sharply by removing packing queues; an extra picker changes nothing because picking is not the constraint.
